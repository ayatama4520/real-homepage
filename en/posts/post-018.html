I Turned the Moon's Craters Into an Ashtray.

Published 2026.03.14

#Essay #AboutMe #Art #Smoking #YasunariKawabata #FranzKafka #YosaBuson

We're all starting to realize that people who say, "I don't even look at social media" now make up the majority of the world. To put it more precisely: sure, people look at it, but it's more that it just streams past them — much like how you don't really remember the scenery flowing by outside a Shinkansen window.

And so, we're also starting to understand, after all, that the only thing that actually means anything is the accumulation of pure, sandbox-child-like creative work. Everyone, all of us, should just be quiet and simply exist, like some old teahouse tucked away in town. Silence is golden.

Content that goes viral melts away in an instant, like a piece of candy swarmed by ants. Our eyes, looking down at that whole scene from above, are unmistakably cold. Or rather — even if we look up, we can't make out anyone's face anyway, backlit as they are.

Looking at society, we've circled right back to ancient Rome. Except the bread and circuses on offer these days are so low-quality it's exhausting. You can't control people's thinking with stuff like that. Human history runs thick. It won't work on a public whose eye has already grown too discerning.

Whenever I lose my way in creative work, I listen to an interview Yasunari Kawabata gave after winning the Nobel Prize in Literature. He says: "Writers can just be scoundrels or street urchins, you know. Every time some honor like this gets added on, it might actually make you regress."

Thank you. Exactly right.

Or I think of Kafka, or Buson. Both of them, during their lifetimes, wrote mountains of work they never turned into books. Had Kafka's friend, or Buson's son-in-law, not taken the decisive step of publishing them, neither would ever have seen the light of day as men of letters.

Art is good. Art is good, but trying to make money off art is, from the outset, simply mistaken. Art isn't a keychain, it isn't a sticker, it isn't a gachapon prize, it isn't the label on a can of beer. Maybe only the ones who've made their peace with it, laughing self-deprecatingly and thinking, yeah, there's no making money off art like this, are actually qualified to call themselves artists. Or maybe that's not true either.

If you happen to be an artist, a poet, and a smoker, say something like: there wasn't an ashtray nearby, so I went and turned the moon's craters into one. The moon sure is beautiful tonight, isn't it.

Plain old littering is, of course, an unmistakably bad thing to do — but artists are, to begin with, exactly that kind of bastard, and precisely because of that, there'd better be something else on top of it, some overwhelming sense of style, to make up for it.

"You Like Jikei, Huh?"

Published 2026.03.04

#Manga #AboutMe #ReadingLog #Bookstores #NatsujikeiMiyazaki

— is what a staff member said to me for the first time at my second-favorite bookstore. Or rather, being spoken to by staff at any bookstore whatsoever was, itself, a genuinely first-time experience for me.

So it took me a moment to switch my brain over into conversation mode. And because I'd only taken one earphone out, my listening capacity had actually dropped to less than half its usual level, so I ended up saying, "huh?" and asking her to repeat herself.

She's a young woman in a blue tracksuit with a hime-cut hairstyle. In the right corner of my mind, I'm thinking about how the roots of the hime-cut trace all the way back to binsogi, the hairstyle of noble women in the Heian period, while in the left corner of my mind, I'm thinking that even though this isn't Village Vanguard, she has exactly the look of a Village Vanguard staff member. And in the very middle of my mind, I'm praying that the "huh" I just made didn't come across as, "please don't talk to me."

"Ah"
"Huh"
"Ah, so you like Jikei?"

Right, I'd just held out a Natsujikei Miyazaki book at the register. "I Probably Won't Be Able to Talk Well With the Girl Who Woke Up From That Dream," which I hadn't been able to find at any other bookstore. And one more thing, which I hadn't even been looking for: Yoshio Matsui's "Sakutaro Hagiwara and the World of Morbid Beauty." That's exactly what's good about this bookstore. Despite being about the size of a storage shed at someone's parents' house, it carves out space for both Jikei and Sakutaro, and makes the admirable effort to loosely connect the two.

"I like Jikei."
"Me too. It really resonates with me."
"I always read it laughing."
"Huh? What kind of feeling is that?"
"I have no idea what's going on, and that's what makes it interesting."
"Is there stuff you relate to, too?"
"There's stuff I relate to, too."
"Huh? But it's good, right?"

My own conversational skills were so terrible, it almost became impressive.

The credit card terminal gleams so purely white it makes you think, isn't this the newest thing in the entire store? I tap my card against it. I wonder if this terminal will ever come to feel like it belongs in this shop. I take the book with a somewhat formal air.

"Sorry, I couldn't put it into words very well."
"No, sometimes not being able to put it into words is actually the better thing."
"I'll come again."

On my way home, I saw a motorbike stopped in the middle of a crosswalk with a green light, with a stream of people flowing around it to avoid it. The rider stares fixedly at some point far in the distance, as if to say, "none of this is my fault, anyway." Families and couples passing by stare at him. And so, he became a rock sitting in the middle of a river.

And it suddenly hit me: this is it. This is exactly what makes Jikei interesting. If she were beside me right now, I could probably explain, "this is it, right here." But I also think she'd probably just say, "huh?" again. Because — I relate to Jikei a great deal too, but I don't think "I relate to it" would ever be the first thing that comes out of my mouth, the way it was hers. Sorry, but honestly, talking about the things you love is just difficult, to begin with.

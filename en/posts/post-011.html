What Even Is Jam Tea.

Published 2026.02.17

#Essay #Poetry #AboutMe #Essays #JamTea

At a coffee shop in Yanaka, the menu lists coffee, café au lait, black tea, milk, cocoa, and then, right after that, jam tea. What is this, exactly?

I order jam tea with the same know-it-all face I'd wear at city hall. What arrives is a hot black tea, along with a small pot of strawberry jam. A glamorous jam, surely made for the sole purpose of being spread on wheat! So, is this saying I'm meant to stir it in? Is this what they call bricolage?

Nervous, I look around the room. At the next table, a middle-aged couple is eating gratin served inside a hollowed-out whole loaf of bread, forced to double as a bowl; further back, a young woman is sipping iced coffee through an impossibly thin straw, like an insect. So, well, in that light, what I was doing wasn't such a big deal either. More than any of that, I just wanted to get back to reading the Desnos poetry collection I'd bought at the secondhand bookstore.

＊　＊　＊

I find something absurd about reading poetry that's been translated into Japanese.

A poetry collection with a list price of 1,200 yen sells for 8,000 yen on Amazon, and 3,000 yen at a used bookstore. This too is absurd.

But that's because poetry itself is absurd, to begin with, and poets themselves are absurd, and above all, this world itself is absurd — so of course it's absurd, plain and simple. Absolutely, thoroughly absurd.

So, that's it, I want to go tearing down the edge of the road on a little yellow bicycle. I want to mistakenly walk into a passport-photo booth thinking it's a purikura machine. I want that entirely innocent girl to throw the portable phone charger she rented from a convenience store into the Sumida River at high noon.

I want to build a campfire in some awful park at night. While playing some cheap programmed beat, you know.

＊　＊　＊

After the jam turned into a memory, I ended up, in the end, feeling satisfied. Down at the very bottom of the cup, its liquid now less than half full, the jam that never fully dissolved has settled to the bottom. Of course it has. I'm glad it turned out that way.

Since only absurdity is truly beautiful, jam tea deserves to become its symbol. Just as the dove is the symbol of peace, and the goddess is the symbol of liberty, I want to build a statue of jam tea in my own garden. In the near future, once I've got some time and money to spare, you know.

The Causality of Jasmine Tea, and Roppongi Station's Elephant Sleep. Also, an Announcement.

Published 2026.01.24

#AboutMe #CreativeLog #Zine #Exhibition #Roppongi #JasmineTea #MatsurikaCha

A plastic bottle rolling around the floor of the last train shows no discretion whatsoever. Wondering what on earth it could be, I squint at it — and of all things, it's an unopened bottle of jasmine tea! Not an empty beer can, not a bottle of mineral water for sobering up!

Jasmine tea is something you almost never pick up unless you have the specific desire, "I want to drink jasmine tea" — which should, by rights, make it somewhat more precious than the drinks I just mentioned. Which is to say, it deserves to be treated with more care, and rolling around on a train floor is simply not befitting of jasmine tea.

For what it's worth, jasmine tea has a Japanese name too: matsurika-cha (茉莉花茶). It sounds like the daughter of a wealthy family — a tomboy, sheltered from the world, but with a certain charm — and I find that a very appealing image. And yet, no one uniformly calls it matsurika-cha. For now, I too am following the crowd and choosing not to call it that.

Now, the jasmine tea's lively rolling looks, at first glance, entirely irregular — but it's actually reflecting some subtlety of physical law that I, merely sitting glued to my seat, can't perceive. It's only through the jasmine tea that this train's speed and tilt finally become visible in a way anyone can grasp.

For instance, when the driver hits the brakes, the jasmine tea goes tearing off toward the wall connecting to the next car like some chattering, boisterous girl, crashes into it, makes a tremendous noise, and jolts all the dozing drunks awake at once (who then promptly fall back asleep).

In fact, this exact thing has been repeating itself, in the same way, over and over since a while ago. While it may not carry quite the same emotional weight as the waxing and waning of the moon or the blooming and withering of flowers, it was, at the very least, just as regular an occurrence. It wouldn't be an exaggeration to say a whole new unit of time had been born, defined by one "drunk-waking" as its basic increment.

So there I was, delighted at how the whole train car was taking on an air of mystery, all because of one rolling bottle of jasmine tea — but in the end, I was born with a short attention span. When the jasmine tea happened to come rolling my way, I firmly stopped it with my foot. As if gently telling it: you can rest now...

And so, one whole causal chain converges, anticlimactically, onto the sole of my foot. The three-dimensional physical law seemed to hold its breath behind a thin membrane, and the clock that had been marking off this new unit of time fell silent as stone. In other words: sleep peacefully, drunks, all the way to Mitaka.

I too decide to bring my attention back to where it belongs. I open the book in my hands — the self-published book I'll be putting out at an upcoming event (for convenience, I'll just call it a zine). Which is to say, this whole piece of writing is about to start taking on the tint of an advertisement.

＊　＊　＊

The Oedo Line platform at Roppongi Station is as deep as an elephant's sleep in the forest. Of all the subway stations in Tokyo, it sits the furthest from the brightness of the surface — like a seven-story building driven into the ground like a nail. It's a depth deep enough that oxygen levels, air pressure, toxic substances underground, or something outright surreal, wouldn't be at all surprising causes of some effect on the human body. Haruki Murakami often writes about unknown entities lurking in Tokyo's subways, and it really does seem like it would be entirely unsurprising if something like that were actually true here.

The official explanation for why it was built this way — "we dug it deep to avoid conflicting with existing subway lines and other infrastructure" — is understandable enough, of course, but I'm certain they must have dug it a little extra deep, just for the mischief of it.

Which means that whenever someone describes a place as "1 minute on foot from Roppongi Station," they're almost never factoring in the time it takes just to get from the platform up to street level, so caution is advised. Anyway, my zine is going to be placed in a stylish shop located in exactly such a spot. Please check the Instagram post below for details.

Since the event's theme is body temperature, I've written up a record of diligently taking my temperature every day since the New Year, along with various other odds and ends. It's a single piece of writing, roughly 16,000 characters in all, somewhere between essay and story, side-stepping back and forth between fiction and nonfiction.

People who read this kind of writing often ask me, "which parts of this actually happened?" — but honestly, that question might be better left unasked, since it kind of kills the mood. That said, I don't dislike it when people ask, and I'm genuinely grateful whenever someone takes an interest. I always want someone to laugh, and I always mean to write things that are actually funny. That's the truth of it.

The cover design is an analog collage by my friend repm. It's a stylish thing, decorated with the motifs that appear throughout the book — a vase, a cat, light, a thermometer.

For the cover paper, I used Cotton Life, bought from Sekaido. Cotton Life is good precisely because it doesn't assert itself much — it has an almost zen-like quality, as if to say, "I am simply myself, here."

For what it's worth, binding with a Gachack (a kind of clip) can't really be called bookbinding. It's basically just a bundle of paper held together. But that's fine, because that vibe is cool in its own way. There's a different kind of goodness to it than having it professionally bound. I'd like to think this isn't just ressentiment talking.

The shop has forty or fifty other zines on display besides mine, the food at Common is delicious, and there's plenty to drink, so I think you'll have a good time. After all, it's Roppongi. There seems to be plenty to do.

So, if you're at all interested, I really hope you'll come by. Thanks in advance.

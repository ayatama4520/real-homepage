A Family Restaurant Full of Single Seats. Some Things I Like.

Published 2026.02.19

#Essay #Fiction #Music #AboutMe #ReadingLog #Essays #Vocaloid #FamilyRestaurant #FamilyRestaurants

There are all kinds of distortions in this world. Karaoke joints that never call to warn you ten minutes before your time's up; fast-food workers who end up more exhausted, not less, once mobile ordering gets introduced; Wi-Fi that flies freely through a café that won't let you stay long; and, above all, family restaurants that have gone and increased their number of single-person seats.

The term "family restaurant" (famiresu) mainly refers to Western-style dining establishments that took root in Japan in the 1970s, aimed at "family" households living in the newly developed suburbs who could drive there casually by car. Rather than the high-end department store dining hall, they were named with the goal of being an everyday "restaurant like home" that an entire family could use. — by ChatGPT

I wonder if the increasingly diversified, individualized shape of the modern household is being reflected in the famiresu too. If my days consist of nothing but picking up the empty husks the times leave behind, inhaling whatever melancholy drifts off them, and converting that into a laugh — well, even so, Gusto's fried chicken is delicious, and the drink bar is unquestionably the best thing ever, so in the end, there's really no problem at all. I'm pouring myself a melon soda as casually as taking off a sock.

＊　＊　＊

The more boring the world within reach of your hand, the more your spirit should spread its body out like a plant. Beauty and mischief like a lotus flower filling an entire pond! A dandelion that's just learned how to fly through the air — that's so earnest it makes you laugh, doesn't it. Lately I keep running into that kind of work. It's such an undeserved stroke of luck that I feel I have to share some of it around.

"I Wanted to Go to That Mountain" / Shiiran

A sharp, non-commercial song. If ad-revenue-first creation is the famiresu, then this song is a small, smoking-allowed, cash-only coffee shop. I won't go so far as to say, "the Niconico Douga of the old days, back when—," but it carries a strangely nostalgic air about it.

There are times when the object of your admiration keeps growing inside your head, gets fatter and heavier, until it becomes unmanageable. But actually, there's no time more enjoyable than the time spent thinking about it, and the song tells you: you, suffering as you keep chasing after it, are beautiful. It creates that feeling of, sure, you fell down in the rain and got covered in mud, but hey, you found a 500-yen coin on the ground — lucky! Like light on a cloudy day. An artist I really want to root for.

"BOXBOXBOXBOX" / Wan Sakamoto

BOXBOXBOXBOX — Wan Sakamoto (Amazon)

The protagonist, standing day after day at the conveyor belt of a fog-shrouded shipping company's cargo depot, doing the same simple task over and over, keeps himself together by fantasizing about what's inside the cardboard boxes. But then, one day, by some chance, he gets the opportunity to actually open one... a mysterious, socially conscious kind of story.

Endless labor binds the body, but in truth, it gives wings to the spirit. What form that gets sublimated into differs from person to person; I just hope, at the very least, it's not a crime. Taking photos, cooking, writing — any of that would do.

In the latter half of the book, there's a scene where the protagonist places an imaginary cactus on his desk. And at that, I thought, "oh, this person's just trying to make someone laugh too, isn't he," and felt relieved. This story, presumably drawn from the author's own real experience, is one the author himself looks down on from above and laughs at as ridiculous. I really love both this author, who can knock his own knees out from under himself, and this story.

＊　＊　＊

When laying things out, there's a rule that three is the perfect number. Even a Mario boss usually goes down after three stomps, and Pokémon lets you pick from three starters. But well, maybe there's value in breaking that kind of pre-arranged harmony, so I deliberately introduced only two things here. It's not that I got tired of writing, or anything.

In the corner of the table, a melon soda gone flat is sleeping. Let me hurry up and drink it and head home. Checkout is self-service. No one thanks me for anything, and I suppose there's no real reason anyone should.
